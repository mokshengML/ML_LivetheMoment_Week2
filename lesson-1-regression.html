Linear & Logistic Regression

Two of the most-used models in the world hide behind every credit decision, every house price estimate, every "you might also like" suggestion. In this lesson you'll play with both — no code, just intuition.

Manager Engineer Social Scientist Curious Beginner
1 The Big Idea

Two questions, two cousins

Linear regression predicts how much. Logistic regression predicts which side. That's almost the whole story.

📈

Linear Regression — predicting a number

You draw the straightest possible line through a cloud of dots, then read off your prediction where it crosses. The line learns from past examples what slope and starting point best match reality.

Example: Maya's bakery wants to predict tomorrow's sales. She gives the model past sales + weather + day-of-week. Output: "Expect ₹14,200 in revenue."
🚦

Logistic Regression — predicting a yes/no

Same idea, but the answer must be a probability between 0 and 1. The line gets bent into an S-shape so it can never escape that range. Above 0.5? "Yes." Below? "No."

Example: A bank gives the model an applicant's income, age, and credit history. Output: "78% likely to repay." → Approve.

7 Check Yourself

Three quick questions

No grades — just a chance to test your intuition. Tap an answer to reveal the explanation.

1Your model has loss 200 at the start. After 50 gradient-descent steps it's still 198. Most likely cause?
Correct: Tiny learning rates mean tiny progress per step. Bump it up — but not so far that you overshoot the valley.
2You have 200 features but only 60 data points. Which model gives you the cleanest, most interpretable result?
Correct: When features outnumber samples, Lasso's ability to zero out coefficients = automatic feature selection. Cleaner story, lower variance.
3A logistic regression outputs 0.47 for a loan applicant. The bank's default cutoff is 0.50. What did the bank do?
Correct: Decisions are deterministic — below cutoff = reject. But banks can adjust the cutoff to be stricter (0.7) for high-risk loans or more lenient (0.3) for small ones.

What to carry away

1
Linear regression predicts a number by fitting a straight line that minimizes squared errors.
2
Logistic regression bends that line into an S-curve so the output is always a clean probability.
3
Gradient descent is just the model rolling downhill, one small step at a time.
4
Learning rate is the step size — too small = slow, too big = bouncing.
5
Ridge shrinks all coefficients smoothly. Use it when every feature might help.
6
Lasso kills weak coefficients to zero. Use it when you want a short feature list.
